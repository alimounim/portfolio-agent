Hermes: Sign Language to Voicesigntovoice.us ↗

HackUTA 2026 MLH Best Use of Gemini API: shortlisted (top 10) Solo build · 24 hours Live

A FaceTime call that translates American Sign Language into a spoken voice, and speech back into captions, so Deaf signers can be understood at a pharmacy, clinic or front desk with no interpreter and no app. You FaceTime Hermes and sign; it says your sentence out loud in natural English and shows the other person's reply as large captions on its video.

HackUTA 2026

  • Built solo in 24 hours at HackUTA's 8th hackathon (Nov 14–15, 2026, UT Arlington), theme: Homer's Odyssey. Hermes is the messenger who carries words between gods and mortals.
  • 402 participants and about 137 submitted projects.
  • Shortlisted by MLH as one of 10 finalists for Best Use of Gemini API. Also entered the Chert FaceTime, ElevenLabs, GoDaddy Registry domain and SpaceXSI challenges.

How I built it

  • Call: the Chert FaceTime API puts the call into a LiveKit room, where my Python agent (LiveKit Agents) joins and streams back a 1920×1080 caption board at 15 fps.
  • Vision: MediaPipe tracks both hands, nose and shoulders. The agent finds where each whole sign starts and ends, then a scikit-learn classifier (PCA + four voting MLPs) labels it from 3,060 body-relative features: hand shape, hand position and movement. It says "not sure" rather than guess.
  • Data: trained on Google's ASL dataset from Kaggle (about 21,000 clips, 21 signers) for a 49-sign vocabulary. On signers the model never saw, it scores 84% top-1 and 95% top-3.
  • Language and voice: Google Gemini (through Google AI Studio) turns ASL gloss like THIRSTY WATER PLEASE into "I'm thirsty, can I have some water, please?", ElevenLabs speaks it into the call, and ElevenLabs Scribe captions the reply.
  • Web: a static site and sign dictionary on GitHub Pages at my own domain, signtovoice.us.
  • Tools: Python, Chert FaceTime API, LiveKit, MediaPipe, OpenCV, scikit-learn, NumPy, pandas, Gemini, Google AI Studio, ElevenLabs, Pillow, Kaggle, GitHub Pages, Cursor, Claude, Grok.

Workshops I attended

  • Intro to Google AI Studio (MLH): prototyping prompts and getting a Gemini API key, which I used to build and tune Hermes' gloss-to-English translation.
  • Your Hackathon A-Team with SpaceXSI (MLH): how to scope, divide and pitch hackathon work, which helped me plan Hermes as a solo builder.
  • Image Processing, by Dr. Marnim Galib (UTA Computer Science and Engineering): how images are represented and processed in code, background for Hermes' video pipeline.
  • Adobe workshop: using Adobe's creative tools for project visuals and presentations.

What's next

  • I'm continuing to build Hermes. Next: letting a signer reach a doctor's office, pharmacy or friend who joins from a browser link, a bigger vocabulary with fingerspelling for names and medications, continuous signing, and testing with Deaf signers.
