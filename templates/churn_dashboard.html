Problem

A telecom company is losing customers: 1,869 of its 7,043 customers (26.5%) churned. Which customers leave, and what could the company change to keep them? I built a one-page Tableau dashboard that breaks churn down by contract, tenure, charges and payment method, with findings and recommendations alongside the charts.

Approach

I used the Telco Customer Churn dataset: one row per customer, with contract type, tenure in months, monthly and total charges, payment method, and whether they churned (Yes/No). Each chart counts customers and colors them by churn status.

  1. Contract Type Churn. Customers by contract type (month-to-month, one year, two year), split into churned and retained.
  2. Tenure Churn Count. Tenure grouped into 5-month bins, to show how churn changes the longer a customer stays.
  3. Total vs Monthly Charges. A scatter plot of total charges against monthly charges, colored by churn. Total charges grow with tenure, so this shows price and loyalty together.
  4. Payment Method Churn. Churned customers counted by payment method.

I combined the four sheets into one dashboard with a text panel summarizing the findings and recommendations, and published it to Tableau Public.

Data notes

  • Churn is a Yes/No text field. Every chart counts customers and splits the count by churn status with color, so churned and retained customers can be compared side by side.
  • Tenure needed grouping. Tenure runs from 0 to 72 months. Binning it into 5-month groups made the drop in churn over time readable instead of 73 thin bars.
  • 11 customers have no total charges. They all have a tenure of 0 months, so they haven't been billed yet and don't appear on the scatter plot.

Insights

  • Contract type is the strongest signal. 42.7% of month-to-month customers churned, against 11.3% on one-year and 2.8% on two-year contracts. Month-to-month customers make up 89% of everyone who left.
  • New customers are the most likely to leave. 54.9% of customers in their first 5 months churned, compared with 3.5% of those who stayed 70 months or more.
  • High prices drive early churn. Churned customers paid $74.44 a month on average, against $61.27 for those who stayed. Long-time customers were much less likely to leave even as their prices rose.
  • Electronic check stands out. 45.3% of customers paying by electronic check churned, compared with 15–19% for every other method. They account for 57% of all churned customers.

Recommendations

  • Bring on new customers at a lower price point and raise prices gradually over time, for a better long-term return.
  • Offer incentives to move month-to-month customers onto one-year or two-year contracts.
